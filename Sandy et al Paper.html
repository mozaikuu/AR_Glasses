📘 Abstract

Background: Smart glasses promise hands‑free ambient intelligence, yet current commercial offerings lack deep multimodal integration, indoor navigation, and personalized AI assistance. Objective: We present CEREBRO, a modular smart glasses platform that fuses speech, vision, LLM reasoning, augmented reality (AR), and indoor positioning to deliver a context‑aware assistant. Methods: The system integrates a YOLO11‑based vision module (custom indoor landmark dataset), Whisper multilingual ASR, an LLM‑driven intent‑to‑JSON task planner, graph‑based A* indoor navigation (manually mapped floor plans), and an ESP32 hardware bridge for smart home control. AR overlays are rendered on a heads‑up display (HUD) using Unity/ARCore. Results: (Preliminary) YOLO11 achieves mAP >90% on indoor landmarks; speech‑to‑action latency <1.2 s; indoor localization error ≤2–3 m. Conclusion: CEREBRO demonstrates a reproducible, edge‑oriented framework for personalized mixed reality assistance, bridging the gap between wearable hardware and advanced multimodal AI.

3.5 Multimodal Speech & LLM Pipeline

Whisper (base model) transcribes Arabic/English speech. Text sent to LLM (GPT‑3.5/4 or local Gemma) with system prompt to output JSON: {"intent": "navigate", "target": "Room 215", "steps": [...]}. MCP‑inspired task decomposition breaks complex commands into executable sub‑tasks.

5. Discussion

CEREBRO demonstrates that a modular, edge‑capable smart glasses system can outperform commercial voice‑only glasses in indoor navigation and contextual awareness. Limitations include reliance on pre‑mapped buildings and LLM latency. Future work will integrate on‑device SLAM and smaller language models for fully offline operation.
